setup

One-time setup for the instructor: connect this page to a free Firebase database so students' phones can sync.

1
Go to console.firebase.google.com → Add project.
2
Build → Realtime Database → Create database → test mode.
3
Project settings (gear) → Your apps → Web app (</>) → copy the firebaseConfig.
4
Paste it into the firebaseConfig block at the top of the script in index.html.

            Risk–return map

            Dots are investors; squares are what each single asset actually delivered this game. The gold line through the T-bill point is the best expected trade-off — a forecast, not a ceiling. In any single game, lucky assets and lucky investors can land above it; run it again and they usually won’t. Diversified portfolios should plot above and left of single stocks.

            Discussion

            1. Compare the wealth ranking with the risk-adjusted ranking. Who swaps places, and why?
            2. MoonShot had the highest expected return on the menu. Look at its geometric growth in Theory corner. Where did the difference go? (Hint: this is the same volatility drag you met in the Kelly game.)
            3. Gold's expected return barely beat T-bills, yet good portfolios held it. What was it being paid for?
            4. Find your dot on the map. What mix of the menu would have moved you up and to the left, toward the gold line?
            5. When the liquidity call hit, who was covered and who got fire-sold? What does that say about holding "lazy" cash?
            6. If everyone's risk aversion γ doubled, how should the winning portfolio change — and which part of it stays exactly the same? (Two-fund separation.)
